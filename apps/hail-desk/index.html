What these numbers are

Hail ≥ 1″
Hail reports of an inch or more in this place, inside the selected window, counted from the individual reports. This is a count of what was reported, not a model. A town with 232 of them is a real signal; a town with two is likely a quiet reporting culture rather than a quiet sky.
Score
A model of the likelihood that a residential structure in this county was hit by damaging hail or wind over the selected window. It blends observed hazard, the FEMA National Risk Index's modelled loss rate, and exposure. Sort by it; do not quote it.
Housing units
Census ACS 5-year table B25001 — how many roofs are in the county. That is the market size, not the number of houses that were hit. The margin of error is shown because B25001 is a sample estimate.
Expected loss / yr
The county's modelled expected annual loss from hail and wind, from the FEMA National Risk Index. A yearly figure, not a dollar amount lost last year.
Max hail
The largest stone ever recorded in the county, in inches. Lifetime, not window — a county that had one softball in 1993 still shows it.

Radar says where hail fell; it is unsound as a measure of how big each stone was, so the size columns come from ground reports and the radar is corroboration, never the measure.

There is no “houses hit” column, and that is deliberate too. Housing units are the market size - how many roofs are in the town - and they are not the number that were hit. Counting the roofs in the swath needs the radar at a resolution that can be laid over a town boundary, which is the next piece of work; until then, a number here would be an estimate dressed as a count.

There is no “reported damage” column, and that is deliberate. The Storm Prediction Center's loss field mixes two different things: dollars for hail, and the estimated population affected for wind. A single Houston wind report carries 70,000,000 in it, and the two are summed into the same county total. A column of those numbers would look like money and mean nothing, so it is not here. The expected annual loss above is the reliable exposure figure, and the housing-unit count is the reliable market size.
